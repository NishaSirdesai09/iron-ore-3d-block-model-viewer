Your plan

What this model answers

Fe shows one iron ore lease as a 3D grid of rock cells. For any part of the ground it answers four questions: is there ore, how rich is it, how sure are we, and is it worth mining or drilling next.

Each cell carries an estimated grade (% Fe), a confidence based on how close the nearest drill hole is, the chance it beats the cut-off grade, and an indicative margin per tonne.

How to read it

  1. Start with Fe grade. Deep red cells are rich hematite; grey is waste or low-grade BHQ. Faded cells have little drilling behind them, so treat their color as a guess.
  2. Switch to Confidence. Bright cells sit near drill holes. Dark ground is unknown, even if the grade view looks promising there.
  3. Check Chance above cut-off. This combines grade and confidence. Cells near 50% are the uncertain ones where one more hole changes the picture most.
  4. Read a drill log. Click any hole to see rock type and assays down its depth, and the best ore interval it cut.
  5. Move the cut-off slider. Raising it shows how tonnage shrinks as you demand higher grade, which is the basic trade-off in planning a mine.
  6. Open Drill plan. Suggested targets are where a new hole would remove the most uncertainty. Add a few and watch how many tonnes move to G2 or better.

The words the model uses

Reef ore

Hard, massive hematite that forms continuous bodies inside the iron formation.

Pocket deposits

Small, isolated lenses of rich ore. Valuable, but easy to miss between holes and hard to plan around.

Blue dust

Soft, powdery, very high-grade hematite, common in Indian iron ore belts.

Lateritic ore

Weathered, iron-rich cap near the surface with moderate grade.

Float ore

Loose ore fragments that have broken off outcrops and rolled downslope. A surface clue to what lies upslope.

Core drilling and RC drilling

These are exploration methods, not ore types. Core (diamond) drilling recovers an intact cylinder of rock for logging and assay; reverse circulation is faster and cheaper but returns chips.

What is simulated

All geology, assays and prices are synthetic and illustrative. Cells are 25 m × 25 m × 10 m, shown with 1.5× vertical exaggeration. Confidence comes from distance to the nearest drill hole; estimated grade wanders further from the true value where confidence is low.
